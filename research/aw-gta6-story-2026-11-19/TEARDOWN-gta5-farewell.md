## Why it works (hypotheses, n=1)
1. **Real people behind the pixels.** Franklin's and Lamar's actors in their own voices, and two creators the gamer feed knows
   (Jelly 23.3M subs, TGG). A non-gamer gets "this man = Franklin" from the stacked split in one second.
2. **Stakes a stranger feels in 3 s.** "It kind of saved me… I was heading in a whole other direction." The game is not a game here:
   it changed a life. That is a belief reversed ("it's just a video game") without anyone saying the belief.
3. **Time as the emotion.** 13 years, a generation that grew up on it ("that game brought me up", "grew up watching the game").
4. **Gratitude, not grief.** Nobody dies; it's a thank-you and a goodbye on the exact eve. The viewer is allowed to feel nostalgic
   for something they share.
5. **A peg.** Posted on the eve of GTA 6: the argument was live (TEMPLATE § 3: "liveness beat lifetime views").
6. **The ending is a voice, not a card.** "GTA V changed my life", held on the song's "again".

## What it does NOT have (and the brief now asks for)
- No AW F1-F7 shape: Shape was WEAK in its own gate table. The viewer gets "it's OK to say goodbye": a feeling, not a lesson or a
  reversed belief. The GTA 6 Short must end in a usable lesson or a reversed belief (VIRAL-BAR gate A).

## What carries over to a GTA 6 launch-day story arc
| Farewell ingredient | GTA 6 equivalent to look for |
|---|---|
| The real person behind the character, own voice | a real person whose own life is IN GTA 6 or tied to its release |
| "It saved me" (stakes beyond a game) | a family, a life, a wait that meant more than a game |
| 13 years / a generation | the 13-year wait (2013 → 2026), the delays, "it's never coming out" |
| Famous faces of the space | the "Michael Jackson" faces: IShowSpeed (streaming), Kai Cenat, the GTA 5 cast |
| The peg (the eve) | launch week itself (16-19 Nov): early access, the first minutes, the first finds |
| Ending on a voice | a line in the person's own voice that closes the loop opened in beat 1 |

**The difference:** a farewell looks back over 13 years of footage that already exists. A launch-day story has only days of
footage, so the arc has to START in the past (a trailer, a delay, a promise, a family story) and PAY OFF in the launch-week footage.
That is the framing to find: an old beat that is proven, closed by a launch-week moment.
