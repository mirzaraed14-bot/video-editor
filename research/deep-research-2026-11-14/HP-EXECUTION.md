## 4. The suspects, ranked by the evidence (none confirmed without § 6)
1. **Viewers read the faces of the two late actors as AI (new in v1.3, scoped in v1.4; the only direct viewer evidence we have).**
   - **The comments:** all 8 are read (the API: 3 threads Sat 21:45, the 5 replies Sun 01:45). 7 of the 8 argue about whether the picture is AI.
     - "have some respect, you are imposing an AI face on two dead men" (12 Nov, 2 likes). Under it: "half this content is ai…" and "you
       dont see the mangled faces".
     - The other side: "This short isnt ai (neither voices nor videos), but youtube puts an ai upscale folter on each YT video…" (13 Nov,
       6 likes), and two replies blaming YouTube's upscaling. One reply asks "Whats the Ai part".
   - **What the build did** (`projects/harry-potter-prank/RUN.md`, `REPORT.md`):
     - Topaz Iris-3 ×4 on the face shots of Dan 2024 (the opening frame) and Rickman 2015;
     - the Gambon shots got Proteus, not Iris (REPORT.md);
     - Proteus ×2 plus a 0.5× Chronos slow-mo on the hook's Snape close-ups;
     - Proteus ×2-×4 on the SD featurette and the dailies, and Proteus ×4 on the 612 % push (a 180×320 crop).

     Its own notes say Iris "invents a waxy face" on small crops, and that Proteus ×4 once turned the dailies "plastic".
   - **The quality cliff belongs here (v1.2's suspect 2):** 0-4.7 s is HD, then a 480p 4:3 picture for 51 % of the runtime. The
     Instagram prank reels ran the same SD untouched, sold as the real take, and it didn't stop them.
   - **Why it may cost views:** a viewer who thinks the faces are fake stops trusting the story, and these are the faces of two actors who
     have died. It's a suspect, not a proven cause (§ 6).
   - **The house rules already cover it:**
     - stills (14 Nov): never upscale a real person's face from a soft source; Lanczos only;
     - footage (LESSONS, 14 Nov, the Speed review): "Iris only where a face is large in frame. A wide or a downscale gets no generative
       enhancement." Affan called the Speed Short's Iris letterboxes "pasty AI-ish", and the fix was Chronos only. That's a related
       complaint about the same family of tools, this time from the creator.
   - **Test:** a retention step in the first 2 s or at 4.7 s, and the comment mix as views grow. Eight comments are a tiny sample.
2. **The hook sold one story, and half the Short told another.**
   - **What the proof was for:** the Instagram proof (291K likes; 173K likes / 88.5K shares) was for the prank, opened as a prank. The
     YouTube proof for "this guy hates me" (1.17M) was titled for the warm payoff ("…very proud of…").
   - **What we did instead:**
     - We opened on fear and titled the fear.
     - We spent 22.5 s (51 %) on a fart prank, told partly by a 14-year-old in SD.
     - Only then, at 27 s and 38.5 s, did we answer the hook.
   - **For the viewer:** the one who came for "he hated me" got a fart joke, and the one who'd love the prank was never promised one.
     It's a softer form of the Hagrid lesson ("just 2 topics"). The prank doesn't answer "did he hate me"; it's a detour.
   - **Test:** a retention dip between about 5 and 15 s, and a stayed-to-watch rate that's fine.
3. **Too many voices for 44 s.**
   - **Six switches**, including one voice nobody can name ("And Dan has asked to have his sleeping bag next to this particular girl…").
   - **What the winners do instead:** the GREEN SCREEN ANIMATION Shorts (6-18M) are one teller each.
   - **Test:** comments asking who's talking, and drops at the voice changes.
4. **The payoff comes too late and arrives in pieces.**
   - "This guy hates me" is answered by Dan's "I think he saw that…" (27.3 s), then Rickman's "Huge pride" (38.5 s, 88 % of the
     runtime), then "I'm so, so lucky".
   - A viewer who leaves before 38 s never hears the reversal in Rickman's voice, which is the one thing nobody else had joined.
5. **The title.** "Radcliffe Thought Snape HATED Him" mixes the actor's real name with a character's name.
   - **To a fan, "Snape hated Harry" is the plot**, so it reads as canon, not as a real-life reveal.
   - The lane's winners mostly use real names and name the payoff or the secret. The title is shown small in the Shorts feed, so this
     matters less than 1-4. It does carry search and the "related" shelf.
   - **Weaker since v1.3:** "How SNAPE Knew the ENDING before the rest of the Cast" (5.65M) uses the character's name and still won,
     because it promises a secret. Naming the setup (hate) instead of the secret is the weaker part of ours.
6. **The topic for THIS audience.** A light prank inside a 💔-style frame, on a channel whose seed audience is trained on MJ-family
   wounds (52 % female, 25-44, US 47 %).
   - Harry Potter itself doesn't need grief: most of its giant Shorts are light (wand injury 8.51M, Felton's worst day 7.06M, "avoid
     watching" 17.93M).
   - What sinks a Short is a promise the body doesn't keep. A warm or wounded HP story in one voice fits AW better than a comedy
     detour.
7. **Smaller items:**
   - **The hook line carries two "like" fillers**, and both are captioned ("for the first like three movies", "just like terrified").
     The line reads slack.
   - **30 fps against the house template's 60.** It shows mostly in the slow-mo and the zooms. Low confidence it moved the swipe rate
     (the Instagram winners are 25-30 fps), but it's an avoidable difference.
   - **The ending was the right kind:** warm, his own words, a hard cut. Not a suspect.

## 7. The HP build checklist (every Harry Potter Short, from now on)
1. **One story, one promise.** The title, frame 1 and the last line are the same story. Write the arc in one line. If the middle is
   another story (a prank inside a pride story), cut it or make it its own Short.
2. **Build to the promise that was proven.** Read how the viral version OPENS (its first-frame text, its caption) and build to that
   promise. Prank proof is comedy proof, and pride proof is warmth proof. Proof doesn't transfer to a different promise.
3. **Frame 1 says Harry Potter.** An iconic film image (the character, Hogwarts) runs under the first spoken line. The adult actor's
   interview frame comes only once the viewer knows the subject.
4. **Real names for real life.** Title the actor (Rickman, not Snape) when the story is about the person, within his 5-word rule.
   Name the payoff or the secret, not the setup. A character's name can work when the title promises a real-life secret ("How SNAPE
   Knew the ENDING…", 5.65M).
5. **Two voices at most:** the teller, plus one answering voice from another era (the birthday join). Every voice is introduced by
   its face or a low label before it speaks, and no voice goes unnamed.
6. **The payoff by about 60-70 % of the runtime**, and the end within about 3 s of the payoff line. Target 25-40 s: the lane's median is
   39 s, and the 8.5M wand Short is 24 s.
7. **No quality cliff in the first 10 s, and no AI faces.** SD stays at 25 % of the runtime or less, unless the whole Short IS that
   footage and is sold as the real take.
   - **Follow the house rule (LESSONS, 14 Nov, Speed): "Iris only where a face is large in frame. A wide or a downscale gets no generative
     enhancement."** Our Snape Short ran Iris-3 ×4 on the Dan 2024 and Rickman 2015 face shots and Proteus on the small crops (×4 on a 180×320 crop),
     and its comments argued about "an AI face on two dead men".
   - Never Proteus ×4 on SD. Use Lanczos, show SD smaller and honest (framed, as the real take), or find an HD source.
     PITCH-CHECKLIST §2's anchor recipe runs Iris 1.0 on large faces, which the rule allows.
   - Check the faces at full size before export.
8. **If each half of a stitch is already viral alone** (the prank 12.5M, "proud" 1.17M), the new value is the JOIN. Put the join
   early, not the halves back to back.
9. **A clean hook line:** no "like" fillers in the captions, and trim them in the audio where the cut is clean.
10. **The house template at 60 fps** (the Son and Jermaine builds), unless a source forces otherwise.
11. **Minors:** HP film footage of the young cast is OK. BTS and press footage of the cast as children is flagged for his call. The
    HBO series' child cast is out.
12. **The AW feeling:** the ending lands warmth or a wound in one line, in the person's own voice.
13. **Show the moment where it exists.** The biggest HP cast reel of the year (1.77M likes / 331K shares) is the moment itself on
    screen. If the story has real footage of the thing (the take, the debut, the reunion), it carries the turn; the interview carries the
    words around it.
