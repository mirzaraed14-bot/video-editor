## What it says (hypotheses, small sample, views not age-matched)
1. **On FAMOUS people, Bolt's two winners run at about half narrator** (NFL 53 %, Ali 45 %): the narrator sets the stakes and
   bridges, and a filmed SCENE with real dialogue carries the turn and the ending. **But the share alone doesn't separate his star
   Shorts.** MrBeast is the lowest share (~42 %) and only 192K. What separates them is the NAME in the hook: Ronaldo (308K) and
   MrBeast (192K) open on it, while the 9.6M hides it ("this NFL legend").
   **Correction (22:00, the full API listing of all 803 uploads, `api-findings.md` § 1):**
   - All-time, a name in the title is neutral: median 2.0M named (n 107) against 1.94M hidden (n 75).
   - Only in 2026 do hidden names win: 1.87M (n 32) against 698K (n 29).
   - Read this point as a 2026 effect, not a rule.
2. **The very high shares (69-100 %) belong to ordinary-people stories**, where nobody knows the face, so the situation and the
   narrator are the whole show.
3. **Amy was 85 %**: the ordinary-people share applied to a famous face, with ~4 s of her own voice. Our cards target
   **40-55 %**. That target comes from AW's own factor (the person or family speaks the wound or the payoff on camera) and the
   mute test (silence the narrator on the climax and the last line; the payoff must survive), not from Bolt's average. The real
   voices belong in a filmed scene (a stage, a confrontation, a moment with dialogue), not only in a sit-down interview.
4. **Every winner ends on the person's own small line** ("I don't feel like a hero", "you better pronounce my name right from
   now on", "That's a 41-year-old who plays football, man"). The narrator never has the last word.
5. Length 40-57 s; ~3.4-3.8 words/s across narrator and source together.
