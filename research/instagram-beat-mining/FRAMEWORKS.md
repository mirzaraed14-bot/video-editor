## What repeats across all of them

1. **The first voice asks or accuses. Nobody narrates.** 10 of the 15 open on an interviewer's question, 5 on an accusation or confession, 0 on a narrator.
2. **The belief can be typed as a comment.** MAGIC-FACTOR's three parts held here: a judgment the viewer already has, the person or family saying it on camera, and a wound that makes the judgment embarrassing.
3. **A physical picture early.** Blotches, umbrellas, matches on toes, kids kissing a body. Never a general statement.
4. **28-48 seconds, median 36.** One idea, no detours.
5. **They end on the sore spot and cut.** No summary, no lesson. Some literally end mid-sentence.
6. **The title carries the role and the wound.** "Michael Jackson's Mother...", caps on one emotional word, then an emoji.
7. **Framing:** tight warm-graded close-up with the eyes visible, big word-by-word captions with one coloured word, archive stills between white lines. (Older thumbnails and birthday-era frames show minors. That rule came later: no minors on frame now.)

## The honest caveat

**Almost all the MJ winners were published 14 May to 18 Jul 2026, in the wake of the *Michael* biopic.** The exceptions are BANNED (5 Sep) and the two October Shorts. So the frameworks and the biopic wave cannot be fully separated. Some of today's weak numbers may be the wave ending, not the shape failing. YouTube also changed how Shorts views are counted around September, so old and new counts are not the same ruler.

**One counter-example to keep in view:** the biggest Short found on the wig story (18.4M) is a narrated cartoon, on a 1.25M-sub channel. So demand for a story can beat the format, and own voice is not the only route to views on YouTube. It is this channel's route, and it is what sets this channel apart.

**A million-view average is not supported by anything in the log yet.** Across the last 18 Shorts on the channel, 2 are over 1M. The frameworks raise the odds; the log does not show them making every Short a million.

## How to use it on a new topic

Before anything is built, every idea answers these in one line each:
1. Which shape, F1 to F6?
2. Write the judgment as a comment a stranger would type.
3. Who says the wound, on camera, in their own voice?
4. What is the physical picture in the first 15 seconds?
5. What is the last line, and does it hurt?
6. Which reels or uploads already prove this beat? (views, shares, link)
7. Has this story at this angle already run on YouTube Shorts, and how many views did it get? Instagram proof alone is not enough: the Bill Bray letter did 4.7M there and at most 153K on Shorts.
