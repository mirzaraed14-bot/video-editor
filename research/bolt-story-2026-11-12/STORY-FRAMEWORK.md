## The framework, in eight rules (with the line from his transcript that shows each)

1. **The first sentence is the whole story's stakes, said by the narrator, about a ROLE, never a name.**
   - "This bus driver had no idea his bus was on fire, and he was about to pull away with 21 kids still on it." (10M)
   - "This man left his mom's house in 1998 and promised her he wouldn't come back until he walked around the entire
     planet." (14M)
   - "This NFL legend wanted to see what would happen if he tried out for a college team against kids half his age." (9.6M;
     Eli Manning is never named in the whole Short)
   - **The two weakest star Shorts open on the name:** "Cristiano Ronaldo wanted to see what would happen…" (308K),
     "MrBeast brought back Squid Game's red light…" (192K). The NFL Short is the SAME disguise idea as Ronaldo's, with
     "this NFL legend" instead of the name: 9.6M vs 308K. Small sample, but it matches AW's own ENGINE §4 ("don't put a
     name in the hook").
   - The Ali Short (2M) opens on 2 s of source audio ("why don't you call me my name, man") and then the narrator: "this
     disrespectful fighter kept calling Muhammad Ali by his slave name". The role is the villain's.
2. **The hook holds a secret the viewer can see and the person can't** ("had no idea his bus was on fire", "nobody noticed
   because she kept swimming on muscle memory", "the only one who knew was the head coach").
3. **The middle is a chain of "but / so / then / until", never "and then".** Each sentence turns the last one: "But he
   couldn't hear her… She ran down the driveway, still yelling, until he finally stopped." "They weren't allowed to jump in
   until a judge said so, and no judge did. But she knew…, so she jumped in fully clothed."
4. **Every beat carries a number or a physical noun.** 21 kids, two minutes, four houses down, the tires exploding, the road
   melting; 28 years, 14 days on ice, 31 days swimming; $800,000, a million dollars, a 92-year-old man; angle grinders, a
   fat suit and a fake beard.
5. **The narrator carries about two thirds to four fifths of the words; the real people get short bursts at the peaks.**
   "Fire, fire." / "What's my name? Pow." / "Chad Powers, you're ineligible." / "30 seconds ago, we were literally
   applauding." The climax is always filmed: the bus going up, the coach in the water, the arena.
6. **The last line belongs to the person, and it's small.** "I don't feel like a hero." "Just so grateful to have her as a
   coach." "And you better pronounce my name right from now on." "That's a 41-year-old who plays football, man." No moral,
   no summary, hard out.
7. **40-57 s, 145-206 words: about 3.6 words a second.** Fast, no pauses over ~0.6 s.
8. **The emotion is a rescue, a promise kept, a trick revealed, or a bully humbled.** Not a wound. The person on screen is
   usually unknown; the SITUATION is the star. Titles are pronoun + situation + "…" + two emojis ("His Bus Driver Passed
   Out…😱🚨", "He Wouldn't Call Him Muhammad Ali", "They Told Him Not To Shoot…😱⚽️").

## What it means for Michael Jackson, Bieber, Kanye and other big stars
- **Bolt's own star Shorts do worse than his ordinary-people Shorts.** On this tab, 4 of his 5 named-star Shorts sit under
  the tab's median (805K across these 48): Ronaldo 308K, MrBeast 192K, IShowSpeed 471K, Ramsay 115K. Ali (2M) is the
  exception.
  The NFL legend (9.6M) worked as a disguise story whose star stays unnamed.
- **So the copyable part is the SHAPE, not "a famous face":** a filmed event with a secret and a number, a role in the hook,
  a but/so chain, the star's own small line to end. A star helps only when his situation is the hook.
- **We already ran this once.** "They Pushed Kanye Off During His Tribute To His MOM 💔" (2 Oct; built from
  `BOLT-FORMAT-CUTS.md` Build A) had an ElevenLabs narrator for about 8 of its 38 s and Kanye's 2008 GRAMMYs play-off as
  the climax. It did **22K (8.8K on day 1, 67.1% stayed)**, inside the channel's normal band. It was not a full Bolt test:
  - narration was about a fifth of the words, not two thirds;
  - the title put the name first;
  - there was no secret in the hook.
- **YouTube's 1 Oct "original content" change** rewards our own narration over re-uploaded clips (`aw-strategy-2026-11-10.md`
  § 2). That is the real reason to test it again, properly.
- **The voice is his call:** his own voice, or one consistent AI voice (the 2 Oct test used ElevenLabs "Oliver Silk").
- **It breaks his source-audio-only rule on purpose.** Say so first in any pitch that uses it.
