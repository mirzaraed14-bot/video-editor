Captions sit conveniently just below the speaker's lips (not on the mouth, not far down the frame), at ONE constant base
position for the whole video. The down-to-up entrance animation is fine; the base position must never shift when the shot changes.

- 2026-10-06 (batch 1 review): *"the distance between the captions and the lips needs to be shorter… I was constantly moving my eyes up and down."*
- 2026-10-07 (batch 1 review 2, after I placed captions per shot and moved them on every cut): *"the actual base positioning of the
  captions themselves just move up and down… I want constant positioning… No videos should have different sets of base positioning."*

**Why:** the eye should rest in one place under the mouth; a caption that changes height on cuts "messes with the viewer's head".

**How to apply:** pick one caption y per video, just under the lips where the face usually sits (`ig_capy.py` constant mode), and make
the FRAMING consistent (face at the same height in every speaker shot) so that one y works everywhere; lay cards out around the caption
band instead of moving the caption. Punctuation and animation rules: [[feedback-caption-punctuation]]. Related: [[project-onyx-sample-shorts]].

**Split formats (2026-10-12):** when the video has split screens, the one constant position is the split LINE, not the lips: see [[feedback-split-screen-white-line]].

**His height (Janet short review, 2026-10-28):** I put the line at 64 % (y1270) to keep one tight, low close-up's lips above it and
placed the letterboxed sources low to match; he pointed with his mouse: "the caption should be here" (y≈1060, 55 %) and "all of
these things are placed too low just because the captions are placed too low". His approved finals measure y940-1130. Start the
line there; move what can move (letterboxes, placed overlays) to put the lips over it; a full-bleed close-up that cannot move
carries the line on its nostril / upper-lip line (he uploaded it that way). A brief's % never overrides his height.

**"Below the lips" is NOT an obligation; never lift footage for it (Conrad Murray review, 2026-10-31):** I lifted full-bleed
faces up to 600 px so captions cleared the lips, and filled the exposed bottom with a stretched blur; he: "this weird artifact
… I haven't set anything in stone that the captions will always be below the lips … you can drag this footage down so the
captions are on his nose … there's no need to forcefully move the footage upwards and have these weird-ass artifacts".
**How to apply:** full-bleed faces stay at their NATURAL framing (picture covering the frame, nothing exposed); the caption
may sit on the nose or mouth. Letterboxed footage moves DOWN so the caption sits ON the person (tie, chest, chin), not in
the empty fill under the band. If a shot is ever dragged down and exposes black at the top, use his letterbox treatment
(white glow line on the picture edge + blurred fill), never black and never a stretched fill.
