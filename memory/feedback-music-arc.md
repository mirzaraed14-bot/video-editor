When he asks for music on a long-form, he wants it chosen "very intelligently", the way he does it himself: analyse the feeling and
vibe of each part of the story. On an emotional/sad film the **beginning is mellow, emotional, sad**; as the topic **builds**, the
music gets **a little intense at some places**; **approaching the end it goes mellow again**. One flat bed for the whole film is not
what he means.

**Why:** BOTH VERSIONS (Paris Jackson) shipped its first cut with one sparse piano bed; his review asked for a scored arc instead.

**How to apply:** map the film's emotional curve per chapter first, then pick tracks per section (Epidemic, measured, no vocals, nothing
bright in the voice band), keep the voice-first levels ([[feedback-voice-first-mix]]), and say in the report which track carries which
section and why. AW code-built SHORTS are the exception: no music bed there ([[feedback-music-is-his-job]]).
