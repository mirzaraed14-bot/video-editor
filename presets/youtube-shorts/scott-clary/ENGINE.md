**The funny test (fixed 2026-11-08, BEFORE any candidate was scored on it).** Score 0-3, one point per part, half points allowed:
- (a) **The laugh:** one line or turn a stranger would laugh at, on the words alone.
- (b) **The scene:** told as events in order, with other people talking or doing something; not reflection or advice.
- (c) **Anyone follows:** a 16-year-old gets it with no industry words; the nouns are things everyone knows.

Proposals need 2.5+. Delivery can't be judged from text, so Affan's ear has the last word on (a). **The cut:** about 42 s or less, ending within about 6 s of the laugh, no advice tail.
**The bar, for the next 3 Shorts, each read at 14 days public:** at least one passes 50K → the test stays and gets 3 more. All three under 13K → it goes to FALSIFIED. Anything between → 3 more before deciding. Chance alone gives one hit in three about 21 % of the time (1 of 13 so far), so one hit is a good sign, not proof.

Bombas did 563K. The other seven did 0.3-13K. These rules are what separates them. They are HYPOTHESES, from one hit plus AW's
three-part factor, and they are scored on every new Short until results promote them or kill them.
1. **Pick test: the famous guest HIMSELF was wrong, lost, or was hurt, about a subject a stranger already knows.** Score each
   candidate 0-3, one point per part (half points allowed):
   - (a) **held belief:** a comment a stranger could type before watching ("a Shark knows what sells").
   - (b) **he speaks it:** the person the belief is about says it himself, on camera, with a face a stranger recognises. A
     semi-known face, or the subject with an unknown face, scores ½. An insider telling a celebrity's story scores 0.
   - (c) **it cost him:** he was wrong, lost, or was hurt, and that reverses (a).

   A win, a brag, a vindication ("they said I'd fail") or advice scores 0 on (c). **A story Claude proposes under 2 goes to the
   bench, not to Affan.** A story Affan picks gets its score written next to it, and the call stays his (his sequencing is followed
   exactly). Evidence: Bombas is the only 3 (563K), and every other Short is ≤ 1.5 (0.3-13K); the per-Short scores are in FINDINGS.
   On AW, three parts gave 1M+ three times out of three.
2. **Never open on the host.** Frame 1 is the guest himself, speaking his own stake line; never Scott's question or compliment with
   the guest listening. Evidence: FUBU vs Bombas, same guest, episode, kit and week, 10K vs 563K, and FUBU opened on Scott's
   compliment. The Serhant calendar opened on Scott's question (5K). A household noun in line 1 is good hygiene but does not separate
   the Shorts on its own: Marvel ("Robert Downey Jr.") and War Dogs ("the film War Dogs") had one and did 5.5K / 13K.
3. **A familiar subject, an untold story.** The freshness search tests the STORY, never the guest. A guest's signature story (the

## The Bombas benchmark (Affan, 2026-11-02: "our record to beat … reference that short with every short idea")
"Daymond John Almost LOST $250M" (`projects/_client-scott-clary/bombas-shark-was-wrong`, 38.7 s) is Scott's most-viewed Short ever
(563K on 10-29, 600K+ by 11-02). **Every idea is scored against it, factor by factor, BEFORE it goes to Affan**, and gets a verdict:
better, on par or worse, plus the gap it would have to close. The scorecard goes in the pick file and in the message to Affan.
| # | Factor | What Bombas has |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | A face strangers know SPEAKS in frame 1 | Daymond John, on his own cam, from 0 s |
| B2 | A superlative or money in his first line | "I have the number one invested Shark Tank product ever" |
| B3 | A held belief, reversed | "a Shark knows what sells" |
| B4 | HE was wrong, lost or was hurt | called the founders "idiots"; his 2-year-old proved him wrong |
| B5 | A familiar subject, an untold telling | Bombas / Sesame Street known by everyone; his telling not indexed |
| B6 | The real event on screen by ~3 s, moving if it exists | ABC's real Shark Tank pitch at 3 s; zero stock, zero data cards |
| B7 | Celebrity and brand nouns all the way through | Flavor Flav, Snoop Dogg, Sesame Street, Elmo |
| B8 | An on-camera emotional or comic turn | the tear that is about money |
| B9 | The payoff is a small physical image, then one line | "Daddy, put on your Elmos" → "it's not for me to object, it's for me to understand" |
| B10 | Tight, one subject from hook to button | 38.7 s, Bombas only |
| B11 | Packaging: title = name + money + loss; cover = a real picture from the Short | "Daymond John Almost LOST $250M"; the sock-wall frame |

Score each ✓ 1 / ½ / ✗ 0 (Bombas = 11). **On par needs B1 and B4, the only two factors unique to the hit (FINDINGS § F1-F6
tested), plus 9 or more of 11. "Better" needs all of that plus something Bombas lacks** (e.g. moving footage of the event inside a
known celebrity's own telling). Anything without B1 is "worse" however high its total, until a Short without B1 passes 100K: then
B1 is re-weighted here, dated. One hit is the basis, so the benchmark predicts direction, not magnitude.
