## 2026-09-16 · shoot conventions, gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee

- **The creator built a natural overlay marker into the performance:** script on a monitor to their LEFT, eye contact
  for the lines that matter, head turned left to read. Eye contact = no overlay; head turned = the frame is free.
  → new tool `workflows/eye-line.py` (head yaw from the five YuNet face landmarks, roll-corrected, scale-free),
  → PLAYBOOK § B "THE EYE-LINE MARKER" (locked). This beats guessing placement off the transcript alone.
- **Validated on synthetic head poses before any footage exists** (the repo had no face footage to test on):
  an 8° head turn separates from lens-facing by 2.6 SD even at 2 px landmark jitter, 20° by ~8 SD, and roll
  (a tilted head) does not leak into the measure. The risk is not the angle, it is a creator using EYES only:
  the five landmarks carry no pupil, so an eye flick is invisible. → the shoot rule says turn the HEAD.
- **A labelled 3 s + 3 s calibration at the head of each take** removes the guesswork entirely (`--calib-camera`
  / `--calib-read` set the threshold from measured means instead of clustering). → PLAYBOOK § B.
- **Dual-system audio is the channel's normal case:** HyperX through OBS for the voice, camera internal as the
  sync scratch. The camera mic must stay ON, and OBS must keep the mic on its own track. → PLAYBOOK § B.

## 2026-09-16 · first real shoot, gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee (32:17 raw)

- **Dual-system sync needs GCC-PHAT, not plain cross-correlation.** A close HyperX and a camera mic
  10 feet away are 34 dB apart and hear different rooms. Ordinary correlation scattered **±50 ms
  (3 frames)**; GCC-PHAT, which whitens the cross-spectrum so only arrival time survives, agreed to
  **0.2 ms across four windows** spanning 23 minutes. → `workflows/sync-dual-audio.py`.
- **Correlate loudness envelopes in dB, never linear.** The first coarse pass compared linear
  envelopes and found no peak at all (1.03x) because it was matching amplitude instead of timing.
  In dB the 34 dB level gap becomes a constant that mean-removal deletes. → same tool.
- **Measure drift, do not assume it.** Two devices at "48 kHz" usually differ by tens of ppm. Here
  they came out at **+0.1 ppm (0.01 frames over 32 min)**, so no resampling was needed — but that was
  a measurement, and the tool resamples when it is not true.
- **Verify the mux, do not trust the command.** The finished file was re-measured against the camera
  scratch at four points: worst deviation **0.19 ms**. Cheap, and it catches a silent filter failure
  before 30 minutes of cutting is built on top of it.
- **The creator shot 59.94 fps, not the 23.976 the playbook specifies** (PLAYBOOK § B). No effect on
  the cut; it does mean no 24p film cadence. Decide before the next shoot.
- **An empty sequence the creator pre-made can be the wrong timebase.** `Sequence 12` was 60.00 fps
  against 59.94 footage. Fixed before the replay — and passing `59.94` set it 1 ppm off, so pass the
  exact `59.94005994005994` and read the ticks back (`4237833600`).
- **Working inside the creator's LIVE project** (`ABW6.prproj`, 102 sequences) rather than a job
  project: scope every write to their named bin and sequence, and read the project back before
  touching anything. → PLAYBOOK § C step 1.
- **On a SCRIPTED shoot the script is a retake-finding tool.** Aligning utterances to the written
  script shows where the creator backed up and restarted; those back-ups are the retakes. →
  `workflows/take-map.py`.
- **A cut sheet rebuilt from word timings can invent duplicates that are not in the edit.** Twice a
  fresh-eyes reviewer reported heavy duplication that did not exist: first because splitting a segment
  copied its whole transcript into both halves, then because a word straddling a cut was counted in
  both neighbours. → assign each word to ONE segment by its MIDPOINT, and self-check for immediately
  repeated tokens before handing text to a reviewer. A reviewer is only as good as the artifact.
- **Verify a text artifact against the audio before trusting a review of it.** The timeline was right
  both times; only the readable text was wrong.
- **The script is the coverage gate.** Diffing the cut against `script.md` caught a real deletion
  ("just turned **off the lock**") and proved the other gaps were only spelled-vs-digit numbers plus
  three passages the creator never recorded. Run it on every scripted job. → `transcript/script-gaps.md`.
- **Auto-dedupe needs an ANCHOR rule:** a retake restarts on the same word. Without it a window slides
  one token off, scores high anyway, and eats the last word of the take being kept. Short spans may
  only skip connectives, or every parallel list ("One is called SEX. One is called KISSING.") reads as
  a retake and loses items. → `workflows/take-map.py` and the job's build notes.

## 2026-09-16 · the hand-cut handoff + reenactment start, gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee

- **The creator's colour labels are the overlay plan, and they are ONLY in the FCP XML.**
  `get_sequence_structure` does not return clip labels — export the sequence with `export_as_fcp_xml`
  and parse it. That export times out client-side while Premiere is still writing, so wait for the
  file to stop growing; it can also be truncated, so parse `clipitem` blocks by regex and split video
  from audio by FILE OFFSET (the video media section ends at the second `</video>`), not by tag
  nesting. → `transcript/overlay-blocks.json`.
- **Label mapping that worked:** Violet = Higgsfield reenactment · Mango = motion graphic ·
  Forest = gameplay · Iris (Premiere's default) = face only, leave alone. Audio clips default to
  Violet and Caribbean, so always filter to the VIDEO track before reading labels.
- **Kling 3.0 is 1.5 credits/second in pro, 1.25 in standard, and perfectly linear** — a 10 s
  generation costs exactly double a 5 s one, so there is no saving in generating longer. Turbo is
  *more* expensive than pro (2.0/s at 1080p). Grok and FLUX are 4.5-5.5/s. Preflight with
  `get_cost: true`, which spends nothing. → `[[reference-higgsfield-connector]]`.
- **A faceless character needs the face described as a SOLID FORM, not as absences.** "No eyes, no
  nose, no mouth" still produced a smoothed face with sockets and a nose in profile. What worked:
  *"a perfectly smooth blank egg-shaped form, like a shop-window dress-form mannequin head... ONE
  UNBROKEN MATTE PLANE from forehead to jaw with ZERO indentations and ZERO protrusions"*, plus an
  explicit line that the SIDE PROFILE silhouette is a smooth curve with no nose. Two credits to fix,
  and it propagates to every shot. → `broll/reenact/character-turnaround-v2.png` is the locked reference.
- **Lock the character with a turnaround FIRST, then pass it as `image_references` to every shot
  still.** One 4-view sheet keeps the figure identical across dozens of generations, which is what
  makes a multi-block reenactment read as one character instead of a stranger each time.
- **A faceless figure must be faceless ALL OVER.** The first cast had blank grey heads on realistic
  pink hands and the creator rejected it on sight: *"their faces are blank and grey but their hands
  are human, it looks weird."* Half-mannequin reads as a rendering bug. → **THE MATERIAL RULE**: the
  whole body is one matte grey mannequin surface (head, neck, hands, fingers, forearms, legs), while
  clothes, hair and jewellery stay fully real in colour and texture. The contrast is the style, and
  it forces all the identifying work onto wardrobe — which is what makes a faceless ensemble legible.
  → `projects/gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee/CAST.md` § THE MATERIAL RULE.
- **A documentary needs a CAST, not a character.** One locked model was never going to cover
  Wildenborg, the modders, the Rockstar higher-ups, the ratings board, two politicians, a senator, a
  grandmother and retail staff. Nine turnarounds cost 18 credits, about two reenactment shots, and
  they stop every later generation drifting. Build the cast BEFORE any shot generation.
- **Real named people are depicted as ROLES, never likenesses.** Every prompt carries "must not
  resemble any real person", the figure is generic, and the on-screen graphic names who it is. The
  facelessness is the safeguard, not a style flourish.
- **🔒 LOCK THE SET THE SAME WAY YOU LOCK THE CHARACTER.** Seven attic shots generated independently
  came back as seven different rooms — skylight in one, roof beams in another, a different wall and
  layout in each. Cut together over a 37-second block that reads as a continuity error, and the
  creator called it before I did: *"where we are doing multiple angles in the same environment try
  your best to have the environment consistent."*
  **The fix: generate ONE master wide of the set first, then pass that image as an `image_references`
  alongside the character reference for every other angle**, with the prompt naming the fixtures to
  match ("same sloped ceiling, same shelf of jewel cases, same desk lamp, same wall colour") and an
  explicit "do not invent new windows, skylights, posters, beams or furniture". The rebuilt six read
  as one continuous room. A set reference is as load-bearing as a character reference.
- **Two references at once work:** character sheet + master plate in the same `medias` array holds
  both the person and the place. Order them plate-first, character-second.
- **`generate_video_batch` can refuse a submission and recommend a PRESET instead** ("IN THE DARK").
  It is not an error and the job is not created — resubmit the same request with
  `declined_preset_id` set to the offered preset id. Pass it preemptively on dark cinematic batches.
- **A SCREEN IN FRAME WILL EVENTUALLY SHOW TEXT — so design it out, don't prompt it away.** Telling
  the image model "no text, unreadable blur" works on the STILL and then the video model re-renders
  legible content on top: a file list became a readable browser window, a 3D viewport became a
  legible modern Blender-like UI (also an anachronism in a 2004 scene). Negative prompting is not
  enough because the screen is still a surface the model wants to fill. **Two framings that are
  structurally safe:**
  1. **BLOW THE SCREEN OUT** — "the camera is exposed for the dark room, so the CRT is a flat sheet
     of pure overexposed white with nothing visible on it". A blown-out screen cannot display text.
  2. **SHOOT FROM BEHIND THE MONITOR** — the dark rear casing and cables in the foreground, the
     figure beyond lit hard by the unseen screen. No screen surface in frame at all.
  Both read better than a blurred screen anyway, and both survived the video stage untouched.
- **Check the LAST frame of every clip, not the first.** Drift is a video-stage failure; the still can
  be clean and the 4.8s frame broken. One contact sheet of end-frames per batch catches it cheaply.

## 2026-09-16 · the autonomous build, gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee

**Reenactments**

- **A TIGHT HEAD SHOT WILL GROW A FACE AT THE VIDEO STAGE, whatever the still looked like.** `b02-b`
  passed still review with a clean blank head and came back from Kling with a full human face — eye,
  brow, nose, lips. The prompt already carried "head angle locked, no facial features ever become
  visible"; it was not enough, because a head filling the frame is a surface the video model wants to
  complete. → **Design the head out of the shot instead of prompting it away** (the same move as
  blowing out a screen or shooting from behind a monitor). The replacement framed the row of figures
  from the chest DOWN — navy torsos and grey mannequin hands, "NO HEAD, NECK OR FACE EVER ENTERS THE
  FRAME, the top of frame stays at chest height and the camera never tilts up" — and it is a better
  shot than the one it replaced. Cost of the lesson: 9.5 credits.
- **The end-frame contact sheet is what catches it.** Three batches of twelve, one sheet each, one
  failure found. Checking first frames would have passed all 37.
- **A generated set will put a REAL-LOOKING SEAL on a government surface** unless the surface is
  named as bare. The hearing-room bench came back with a gold eagle roundel. The prompt had said
  "no seals"; what worked was making the surface itself the subject: *"THE FRONT OF THE PANEL BENCH
  IS COMPLETELY BARE POLISHED WOOD PANELLING - there is no seal, no crest, no roundel, no eagle, no
  medallion, no emblem, no plaque and no device of any kind mounted on it or on the wall above it."*
  Describe the plane, not the absence — the same rule as the blank head.
- **"One figure alone" needs saying twice.** An over-the-shoulder prompt gave the model a reason to
  invent a second person to shoot over. *"ONE SINGLE PERSON IS IN THE ROOM AND NOBODY ELSE"* plus a
  named empty chair fixed it.
- **A master plate per set, reviewed before any angle, held for all ten new sets** — zero continuity
  retakes across 37 shots, against seven different rooms the first time this was skipped.

**Motion graphics**

- **THE RACK IS A CROSSFADE, NOT A FADE-UP.** Raising `#racked` to opacity 1 leaves `#content` fully
  opaque underneath, and the racked field is only ~60 % black, so the SHARP scene reads straight
  through the blurred one and the payoff type lands on a legible background. Every racking comp came
  back wrong the same way. → fade `#content` to 0 on the same beat, and deepen `#racked .field` to
  0.72. Owner: the job's `hf-graphics/gfx/base.css` + each comp.
- **A `filter` on an ancestor FLATTENS 3D transforms.** g10's rating plate flipped correctly in the
  sharp scene and showed the mirrored front face in the blurred racked clone, so the card still read
  M under a sentence about the AO re-rating. → swap faces by OPACITY with a scaleX squeeze; it reads
  as a flip and survives the blur.
- **The racked clone keeps the same ids, and `querySelectorAll('#id')` returns BOTH copies** — so one
  call animates the scene and its clone together. Driving the clone separately double-applies every
  tween.
- **`&nbsp;` between words means the line CANNOT wrap**, so a long line runs off the frame instead of
  breaking. Use real spaces inside a fixed-width box and let it wrap. Caught by a luminance-threshold
  edge check, not by eye.
- **An alpha bbox cannot find overflow on a bg-dark full-screen** — the near-black field fills the
  alpha channel. Threshold on LUMINANCE instead: on this palette everything the eye reads as content
  is far brighter than the field. → `hf-graphics/gfx/edgecheck.py`.
- **`.o` must not force `position:absolute`.** An inline type span taken out of flow cannot be centred
  by `text-align`, and the line silently starts at frame centre and runs off the right edge.

**Premiere**

- **Placement paths must be normalised before comparison.** Premiere returns Windows paths with
  backslashes; a placement file carries forward slashes, so every `findByPath` missed and every import
  looked like a failure. Compare lower-cased with separators normalised — and build the normaliser
  with `String.fromCharCode(92)`, because a backslash literal does not survive the trip through
  Python string escaping into ExtendScript.
- **`setOutPoint` quantises to the SOURCE frame grid.** A 24 fps Kling clip in a 59.94 sequence landed
  up to 3 timeline frames short of its slot. → over-trim by 0.2 s and ALWAYS pin `clip.end`
  afterwards, in both directions. A short clip on V2 is a hole, and a hole on V2 flashes the face.
- **The reenactment layer needed its own owner.** `place-graphics.py` owns V3/V4; this channel's
  second picture layer is now `lanes/premiere/place-reenactments.py`, with a HOLE CHECK in `--verify`.

**The cut**

- **The creator's hand cut is the canonical transcript, and the pipeline's artifacts have to say so.**
  `outputs/*.transcript.json` and `transcript/cuts.json` still described my rough cut (792.9 s) after
  the creator re-cut to 727.9 s, so `validate-plan.py`, `sfx-plan.py` and every anchor check would
  have been measured against a timeline that no longer exists. Rebuild both from the hand-cut XML by
  MIDPOINT word assignment and keep the old pair as `*.roughcut-v1.json`.
- **Diff the DELIVERED READ against the script, not just the cut against the script.** Two dates are
  spoken wrong (2:27 "July the 17th" for January the seventh; 5:18 "the 17th of July" for the
  seventh), with no alternate take. Graphics were then built so that no card states a contested date,
  and the errors went to the creator as a decision rather than being papered over.

## 2026-09-16 · what the fresh-eyes review caught, gta-san-andreas-hot-coffee

Two subagents ran the step-5c review against the built comps, the plan, the preset and `FACTS.md`.
The placement review came back clean on all eight checks. The graphics review found one serious
defect and one real timing bug — both of which every earlier check had passed over.

- **A CARD CAN STATE A LEGAL FACT BACKWARDS AND STILL PASS EVERY OTHER GATE.** g15 highlighted
  "REGARDLESS OF WHETHER A THIRD PARTY WAS THE ONE WHO ENABLED IT". `FACTS.md` C6 says the FTC
  order's Definition 7 is "regardless of whether **respondents** have disabled it for game play",
  and Definition 4 **EXCLUDES** games altered by third parties — the card said the opposite of the
  document. It got there because the narration says it, the plan cell repeated the narration, and
  the build faithfully rendered the plan. `validate-plan.py` cannot catch this: it checks copy
  against a source file, not a claim against the fact-check.
  → **THE FACT GATE HAS TO RUN AGAINST THE BUILT CARDS, not just against the script.** The fix is
  the same move as the contested dates: the card now carries only the FTC's own verified wording for
  what counts as content and drops the clause, so it is true AND does not contradict the voice.
- **`riseIn`'s third argument is DISTANCE, not duration.** The rise always runs `REG.rise.in.dur`
  (0.55 s), so any entrance started less than 0.55 s before `DUR` is still moving when the hard cut
  comes. g18's payoff line was 69 % through its rise at the cut; g7's censored row 82 %. Nothing
  measures this — `graphics-qa.py` checks dynamics and racks, not whether an entrance finishes.
  → pass an explicit 4th argument for any entrance inside the last 0.6 s of a comp.
- **A plan and a timeline must never disagree, even when the build is RIGHT.** g9 shipped "LEFTOVER
  MATERIAL FROM A CUT IDEA" where the plan said "LEFTOVER MATERIAL"; the built line is the more
  faithful one, so the PLAN moved to match the build rather than the difference being left to rot.
- **Not every finding is a defect.** The review also flagged the chapter card "SIX DAYS" as carrying
  a one-day date drift (`FACTS.md` B3: the statement ran 13 July, not 14). It is a chapter title that
  repeats the script's own section name and the narration; changing it would CREATE a contradiction
  where there is none. It stays, and it goes to the creator with the other date issues.
- **`place-graphics.py --remove` silently matched nothing on Windows**: it took the basename with
  `p.split("/").pop()`, and `getMediaPath()` returns backslashes, so `b` was the whole path. Third
  time this same bug class has cost a session — **normalise the separator on every path Premiere
  hands back**, at every call site.

## 2026-09-17 · THE CREATOR'S REVIEW — the placement was the failure

  `make_reenact_placement.py` took each Violet block, split it into `ceil(len / clip_len)` EQUAL

  the delivered cut:

    is ON THE SCREEN; the shot is the back of his head.
  - 04:02.53 *"The Netherlands gets San Andreas on PC three days after North America"* → the attic
    DOORWAY, then the EMPTY ROOM. A line about a release date, under a shot of nobody.
  - 10:36.85 *"GTA Garage took Hot Coffee down"* → over-the-shoulder, then the empty room again.

## 2026-09-17 · finishing the re-anchored pass

- **"Placed" is not "verified" until it is read back from the PROGRAM MONITOR.** Half the re-anchored
  rows were built, rendered, logged and reported before a dropped bridge meant they were never on the
  timeline at all. The creator found it, not me: *"where is the Best Buy Walmart Circuit City … those
  are not present."* A build report that lists what was RENDERED reads exactly like one that lists
  what was PLACED. → say which, and prove placement with frames off the timeline.
- **A hole check needs real run boundaries.** Every v3 row carried `block: 0`, so the check treated
  the whole film as one contiguous run and reported all eight ordinary Iris gaps as holes — eight
  false alarms next to a genuine all-clear. A check that cries wolf is worse than no check: number
  each contiguous run before trusting it.

- **THE CREATOR PLACES SOME OF THEIR OWN SOUND. READ THE TIMELINE BEFORE PLANNING ANY.** On Hot
  Coffee they had already hand-laid bank-terminal beeps across the hex-editor shots, a designed
  press-conference ambience across the whole press run, the desk phone, three rubber stamps and hall
  room tone in the courtroom. A plan built from the picture alone would have doubled every one of
  them - which is the "sounds weird" they asked to have fixed, not the fix. 16 of 75 planned cues
  were dropped as duplicates. **De-duplicate by SOUND CLASS, not by track**: their diegetic sound and
  the graphics transients share A4, so vetoing on any overlap kills good beds.
- **THE HIGGSFIELD OVERLAYS WANT A ROOM, NOT AN EFFECT.** *"scan the actual overlay, look what the
  environment is - if there's a computer maybe beeping sounds, if it's a courtroom maybe subtle crowd
  sound."* Pull a frame from each overlay and read the room off it; the file name is not enough.
  Nine environments cover this channel's reenactments: ROOM_NIGHT, OFFICE, CHAMBER, HEARING,
